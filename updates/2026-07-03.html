Message for Leo — copy & send

Hi Leo — we audited the plumbing campaign over the last two weeks. The ads are producing real, high-intent calls: for example a 155-second call in Boise and a 223-second call in Denver where customers stayed on the line, plus a Denver caller asking specifically for trenchless main sewer line repair, which is a high-ticket job. The issue is that most of these calls are being lost on the call-handling side, not on our end. In one recording the agent told the sewer-repair caller they were "just an answering service" and the customer hung up. Another caller dropped off at the automated menu while being asked to enter a zip code before reaching a person. And three calls from a local Colorado number were blocked outright by the STIR/SHAKEN spam filter. Separately, the Ringba call-conversion feed into Google has not been reporting, so Google has been bidding blind — we're going to take over conversion tracking on our side from now on so that is no longer a dependency. Before we relaunch, we'd need your team to tighten the agent script so they don't describe themselves as an answering service, drop or shorten the zip-code menu step so emergency callers reach a person faster, and review the STIR/SHAKEN filter so legitimate local calls aren't blocked. Once that is in place and the next deposit is settled, we'll switch everything back on.

Why so few calls — the short version

  • Google was bidding blind. Only 1 call ever reached Google as a conversion (Ringba's feed is broken), so it just spent budget — that's how Jun 29 hit $861 for 0 qualified calls. We now track conversions ourselves.
  • The good calls die on pickup. Real 155s and 223s calls prove the traffic works — but the agent "answering service" line, the zip-code menu, and the spam filter kill the rest. All on Leo's side.
  • The math: ~$1,680 spent → 3 qualified/ad-linked calls ≈ $560 per good call. Not sustainable as-is.
  • Pausing stopped a bleeding account — it didn't lose a winning one.
